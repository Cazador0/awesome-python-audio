# Melody and motif

## The motif as the unit of identity
A **motif** is the smallest recognizable melodic-rhythmic cell — often 2–5 notes.
Memorable melodies are economical: they state a motif and then *develop* it rather than
introducing new material continuously. Core development techniques:
- **Repetition** — exact restatement (cheap, powerful; pop tolerates more than you think)
- **Sequence** — restatement at a different pitch level (up a step is the most common)
- **Inversion** — mirror the contour
- **Augmentation / diminution** — stretch or compress the rhythm
- **Fragmentation** — develop just the head or tail of the motif (the engine of
  build-ups)

A melody whose every bar is unrelated to its neighbors scores low on coherence in
[[best-of-n-evaluation]]; one that repeats a single cell without variation scores low on
development. The craft is the ratio.

## Phrase structure
Two classical templates still govern popular melody:
- **Period**: antecedent phrase ending on a weak cadence, consequent answering with a
  strong one (question–answer, typically 4+4 bars). See
  [[harmony-and-voice-leading]] for cadence types.
- **Sentence**: statement (2 bars) → varied restatement (2) → continuation/fragmentation
  driving to cadence (4). The sentence's built-in acceleration makes it the natural
  pre-chorus shape.

Phrase lengths default to powers of two; a deliberate 3- or 6-bar phrase is a hook
*because* it violates the default (see [[rhythm-and-meter]] on grouping dissonance).

## Contour, range, tessitura
- **Contour** is what listeners retain first: arch (rise–peak–fall) is the most common
  satisfying shape; a single peak per phrase, placed late, maximizes payoff.
- **Range** (total span) vs **tessitura** (where the line *sits*): verses sit low,
  choruses sit high — the tessitura lift, more than loudness, is what makes a chorus
  "open up". For vocals, keep within ~a 10th per section and respect the
  [[orchestration-and-timbre]] register of the voice class.
- Stepwise motion should dominate, with leaps (≥ a 4th) used as accents and ideally
  recovered by step in the opposite direction.

## Hooks
A hook is a motif engineered for retrieval: strong rhythmic identity (it survives being
clapped), placed at the section's tessitura peak, repeated at fixed formal positions
(see [[form-and-structure]]), and often gapped with silence so it can echo. In caption
terms ([[genre-tag-taxonomy]]), "catchy lead", "anthemic vocal hook" steer the engine
toward this behavior — but verification is contour/repetition analysis on the rendered
stem, not the caption.

## Evaluation criteria
Motivic recurrence rate per section; one clear contour peak per phrase; tessitura
contrast verse→chorus; leap-recovery ratio; melody/bass independence (see
[[harmony-and-voice-leading]]).
